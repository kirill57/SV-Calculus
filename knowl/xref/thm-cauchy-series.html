Theorem 15.2.20 Cauchy criterion for real series.

The series \(\sum_{k=1}^\infty a_k\) converges if and only if, for every \(\varepsilon>0\text{,}\) there is an integer \(N\) such that
🔗
\begin{equation*} \left|\sum_{k=p}^q a_k\right|<\varepsilon\qquad\text{whenever }q\ge p\ge N. \end{equation*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Use \(\sum_{k=p}^q a_k=S_q-S_{p-1}\text{.}\) The Cauchy condition for \((S_n)\) implies the displayed condition after increasing the starting index by one. Conversely, if two partial-sum indices are sufficiently large, their difference is either zero or a tail of this form. Thus \((S_n)\) is Cauchy and converges.
