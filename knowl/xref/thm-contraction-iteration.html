Theorem 15.10.1 Contraction mapping theorem.

Let \(D\) be a nonempty complete metric space and let \(T:D\to D\) be a contraction with constant \(q<1\text{.}\) There is exactly one fixed point \(x_*\in D\text{.}\) For every starting point \(x_0\in D\text{,}\) the iteration \(x_{n+1}=T(x_n)\) converges to \(x_*\text{.}\) For \(n\ge1\text{,}\)
🔗
\begin{align*} d(x_n,x_*)\amp\le\frac{q^n}{1-q}\,d(x_1,x_0),\\ d(x_n,x_*)\amp\le\frac{q}{1-q}\,d(x_n,x_{n-1}),\\ d(x_n,x_*)\amp\le\frac{d(x_n,T(x_n))}{1-q}. \end{align*}
🔗
🔗

Proof.
Repeated contraction gives \(d(x_{k+1},x_k)\le q^k d(x_1,x_0)\text{.}\) For \(m>n\text{,}\) the triangle inequality and the finite geometric sum give
🔗
\begin{equation*} d(x_m,x_n)\le\sum_{k=n}^{m-1}q^k d(x_1,x_0)\le\frac{q^n}{1-q}d(x_1,x_0). \end{equation*}
🔗
Thus the iterates are Cauchy and have a limit \(x_*\in D\text{.}\) The contraction inequality implies continuity of \(T\text{,}\) so taking the limit in \(x_{n+1}=T(x_n)\) gives \(x_*=T(x_*)\text{.}\) Two fixed points would satisfy \(d(x_*,y_*)\le qd(x_*,y_*)\text{,}\) forcing their distance to be zero.
🔗
Letting \(m\to\infty\) in the finite-tail bound proves the first estimate. Starting the same geometric bound at the most recent step proves the second. For any \(x\in D\text{,}\)
🔗
\begin{equation*} d(x,x_*)\le d(x,Tx)+d(Tx,Tx_*)\le d(x,Tx)+q\,d(x,x_*). \end{equation*}
🔗
Rearranging proves the residual estimate, including the third bound. If \(q=0\text{,}\) the map is constant and one step already reaches its fixed point.
