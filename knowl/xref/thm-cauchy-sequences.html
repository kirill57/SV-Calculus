Theorem 15.1.26 Cauchy criterion for real sequences.

A sequence of real numbers converges to a real number if and only if it is Cauchy.
🔗
🔗

Proof.
If \(a_n\to L\text{,}\) choose \(N\) so that \(|a_n-L|<\varepsilon/2\) for all \(n\ge N\text{.}\) The triangle inequality proves the Cauchy condition.
🔗
Conversely, a Cauchy sequence is bounded: take an eventual distance bound of \(1\) from one fixed term and then include the finitely many earlier terms. Define the tail bounds
🔗
\begin{equation*} \ell_N=\inf\{a_n:n\ge N\},\qquad u_N=\sup\{a_n:n\ge N\}. \end{equation*}
🔗
Here \(\inf\) means greatest lower bound and \(\sup\) means least upper bound; completeness of the real line ensures both exist. The sequence \(\ell_N\) increases and \(u_N\) decreases, and both are bounded. The Monotone Convergence Theorem gives their limits. The Cauchy condition implies \(u_N-\ell_N\to0\text{:}\) if every pair of tail terms has distance less than \(\varepsilon\text{,}\) the tail’s supremum minus its infimum is at most \(\varepsilon\text{.}\) Their limits are therefore the same number \(L\text{.}\) Since \(\ell_N\le a_N\le u_N\text{,}\) squeezing gives \(a_N\to L\text{.}\)
